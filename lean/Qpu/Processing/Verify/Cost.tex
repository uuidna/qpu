\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.digest\_collision\_at\_category\_two}}
Its collision strength sits at the category-2 floor (128-bit classical birthday).
\[ \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.collision}\ 256 = \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.category2} \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L38}

\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.digest\_preimage\_at\_category\_five}}
SHA-256 preimage strength sits at the category-5 floor (256-bit classical).
\[ \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.preimage}\ 256 = \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.category5} \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L35}

\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.forging\_costs\_more\_than\_verifying}}
Forging costs more than verifying, for any nonempty width and seam count: the asymmetry that makes receipts cheap to check and expensive to fake.
\[ \forall (\mathrm{bits}\ \mathrm{seams} : \mathbb{N}), \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.verify} \le \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.chained} (\mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.preimage} (\mathrm{bits} + 1)) (\mathrm{seams} + 1) \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L60}

\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.sha224\_collision\_below\_category\_one}}
SHA-224 is the near miss: 112-bit collision strength is below category 1, the level NIST deprecates after 2030.
\[ \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.collision}\ 224 < \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.category1} \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L52}

\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.the\_anchor\_holds}}
Control: a full-width digest chained once does clear category 2 — the refusals above are not vacuous.
\[ \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.category2} \le \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.chained} (\mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.collision}\ 256) 1 \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L56}

\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.three\_uuid\_seams\_clear\_category\_one}}
Three clear the floor — that is what multi-seam must mean to be worth saying.
\[ \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.category1} \le \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.chained} (\mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.collision}\ 122) 3 \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L48}

\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.two\_uuid\_seams\_are\_still\_short}}
Even two UUID seams stay short: 61 + 61 = 122 < 128.
\[ \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.chained} (\mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.collision}\ 122) 2 < \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.category1} \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L45}

\paragraph{\texttt{Qpu.Verify.Cost.uuid\_handle\_collides\_below\_category\_one}}
A 122-free-bit UUID handle collides at 2⁶¹ — below category 1. A bare address is not a security boundary.
\[ \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.collision}\ 122 < \mathrm{Qpu.Verify.Cost.category1} \]
\noindent axioms: none; proof: \url{https://github.com/uuidna/qpu/blob/v1.1.1/lean/Qpu/Processing/Verify/Cost.lean\#L42}
